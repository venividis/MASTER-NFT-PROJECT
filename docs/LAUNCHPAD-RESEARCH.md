# ANIMA Launchpad: Research, Source Findings and the Operational Rebuild

Research and implementation snapshot: **13 September 2026**. This report combines inspection of the uploaded ANIMA source, primary protocol documentation and code, and newly executed local integration tests. Source filenames are relative to the project root. “Archive” means the supplied project; “new edition” means the implementation being assembled in this task. The new transaction adapters and their unified graphical interface have been implemented and locally tested. The canonical NFT build includes the interface and its deployment artifacts. No public-chain deployment or automatic upgrade of an already minted NFT is claimed.

**The previous launchpad stopped at the wrong layer.** The uploaded project already contained real token issuance, fundraising, liquidity, trading, fee routing, vesting and transaction preparation. The earlier studio concentrated on configuration and mathematical exploration. Its exported plan identified itself as `configuration-only`; its pool still selected the zero hook address even when a separate fee-flow design was enabled. Those were concrete missing connections between the interface and available contracts. A more atmospheric form could not close that gap. The appropriate correction is an operational launchpad that creates an identifiable launch, supports the people funding it, settles its outcome, and lets everyone exercise the resulting rights.

A wallet-connected website can prepare and submit real Ethereum transactions. The NFT does not need to invent another blockchain or replace the AMM. It needs a verified contract deployment, correct transaction construction, funding and spending authorization, the appropriate owner or participant signature, and receipt reconciliation. Where infrastructure is absent, the application can prepare its deployment through the same wallet. Where it already exists, it should verify and use it. Source availability is enough to build those paths; it is not evidence that a particular address is already deployed or that a user has signed a launch.

**The fundamental unit is a launch with continuing obligations.** A launch creates an asset and a set of promises: who can contribute, how contributions become allocations, what happens if demand is insufficient, where proceeds go, who owns liquidity, when tokens become available, and who can alter future fees. The most important design advance is to carry those promises through every stage. This is a synthesis of the responsibilities visible in Uniswap’s strategy system, Doppler’s modular launch architecture, Gnosis auctions and established sale workflows. [Uniswap liquidity strategies](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/liquidity/liquidity-launchpad/concepts/liquidity-strategies), [Doppler Airlock](https://github.com/whetstoneresearch/doppler/blob/main/docs/Airlock.md), [Gnosis auction contracts](https://github.com/Gnosis-Auction/auction-contracts/blob/main/README.md).

| Phase | Creator needs | Participant needs | Evidence that the phase completed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design | Asset, mechanism, allocations, prices, recipients, dates and permissions | Readable rules and token powers | Validated terms tied to a particular mechanism |
| Prepare | Infrastructure, inventory, liquidity budget and spending rights | Eligibility, accepted asset and contribution bounds | Verified contracts and exact funding requirements |
| Create | Submit the chosen contract operation | A stable address or launch identifier | Successful receipt and matching creation event |
| Participate | Observe funding and exercise permitted controls | Contribute or bid, inspect allocation, withdraw when permitted | On-chain contribution or standing-order state |
| Resolve | Settle success, failure or cancellation | Learn what was accepted and what is refundable | Contract status and conserved balances |
| Establish market | Fund liquidity, identify its custodian and handle leftovers | Inspect actual trading depth and withdrawal rights | Funded pool and actual liquidity position |
| Distribute | Release permitted treasury or team balances | Claim purchased tokens and unused funds | Individual claimable and already claimed amounts |
| Operate | Manage permissible liquidity and fees | Trade and exercise continuing rights | Positions, fee claims, schedules and transaction history |

The interface should always answer “What exists now, what do I own, and what can I do next?” A pending transaction is a submitted instruction. A configured pool is a proposed market. A funded position is actual liquidity. Those distinctions should be expressed through useful states and actions rather than intrusive badges attached to the artwork.

**Conservation is the first mathematical test.** Let total token supply be \(S\). Let the final nonoverlapping allocations be sale, liquidity, team, treasury, community and other allocations. Then

\[
S=S_{sale}+S_{LP}+S_{team}+S_{treasury}+S_{community}+S_{other}.
\]

For deposited funding \(C\), accepted funding \(A\) and refundable funding \(R\),

\[
C=A+R,\qquad A=A_{LP}+A_{treasury}+A_{fees}+A_{other}.
\]

A vesting claim is a restriction on an existing allocation, not additional supply. A token’s fully diluted valuation is price multiplied by supply; it is neither capital raised nor money available for withdrawals. Virtual reserves describe a pricing mechanism, not spendable collateral. Every visualization and transaction plan must preserve these meanings. Integer rounding belongs in the accounting model: final claims cannot change because another participant claimed first.

**Mechanism selection must change the actual rules.** A fixed sale, a pro-rata raise, a streaming auction and an immediate liquidity launch answer different questions. Calling all of them “launch” is convenient navigation, but the selected contract determines their rights and outcomes.

| Mechanism | What determines allocation or price | Useful characteristic | Essential distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed price | A predefined exchange rate | Easy contribution budgeting | A scarce hard cap may create a first-arrival race |
| Overflow fixed price | Fixed price with accepted contributions prorated after oversubscription | Removes dependence on filling the cap first | Larger capital still receives a larger allocation |
| Uniform batch auction | Funded limit orders settle at a common clearing price | Explicit price discovery | Cancellation, marginal fills and refunds require their own rules |
| Continuous or streaming auction | Issuance over time interacts with persistent demand | Distribution spans multiple moments | The exact schedule and clearing algorithm matter |
| Bonding curve | Price responds to inventory or supply | Continuous buys, sometimes sells | Reserves, minting, sellback and migration must be specified |
| Weight-shifting LBP | Actual balances and scheduled weights | Can reduce initial reserve funding | The no-trade price path is not a forecast |
| Direct v4 launch | Creator selects starting price and funds a range | Immediate, real market creation | It does not create a fundraising campaign by itself |

These comparisons are engineering synthesis. Fixed and LBP workflows are documented by Fjord; uniform funded orders by Gnosis; weighted pricing by Balancer; continuous clearing by Uniswap. They do not establish that every method is integrated into ANIMA. [Fjord sale types](https://help.fjordfoundry.com/fjord-foundry-docs/for-sale-creators/token-sale-types), [Gnosis mechanism](https://github.com/Gnosis-Auction/auction-contracts/blob/main/README.md), [Balancer weighted math](https://docs-v2.balancer.fi/reference/math/weighted-math.html), [Uniswap CCA paper](https://developers.uniswap.org/whitepaper_cca.pdf).

**Current Uniswap CCA is a useful benchmark, with a version boundary that matters.** Its bids contain a funding budget and maximum acceptable price; issuance is distributed across a schedule. The repository recommends **v2.1.0**, pinned to commit `7d7602d257733315434570f2a0c2f94f1c7b207a`. It identifies that version as v2.0.0 plus an Arbitrum/Orbit correction and links June 2026 audits of v2.0.0. That is external protocol provenance, not an audit or deployment of ANIMA. [Official CCA repository](https://github.com/Uniswap/continuous-clearing-auction).

The lifecycle goes beyond bidding. Active, outbid and partially filled bids have different exit conditions. Exiting resolves unused currency; claiming purchased tokens is another operation. Graduation, final checkpointing and the liquidity strategy determine when the auction can hand off to a pool. Failed graduation permits recovery under the actual auction rules. The ANIMA interface must never represent these as one generic claim button if integrating that protocol. [Uniswap exit and claim guide](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/liquidity/liquidity-launchpad/guides/exit-bid), [Pinned CCA implementation](https://github.com/Uniswap/continuous-clearing-auction/blob/7d7602d257733315434570f2a0c2f94f1c7b207a/src/ContinuousClearingAuction.sol).

An important research finding is documentation drift: a technical example still describes `initializeDistribution()` and `getAuctionAddress()`, whereas the recommended factory implements `create()` and `getAddress()`. Its changelog records the rename and rollover changes. A state-of-the-art integration requires pinned code and its matching ABI; copying a recognizable documentation snippet is insufficient. ANIMA’s existing contract with a similar auction name is a different implementation and must retain its own label. [CCA technical documentation](https://github.com/Uniswap/continuous-clearing-auction/blob/main/docs/TechnicalDocumentation.md), [Pinned factory](https://github.com/Uniswap/continuous-clearing-auction/blob/7d7602d257733315434570f2a0c2f94f1c7b207a/src/ContinuousClearingAuctionFactory.sol), [Pinned changelog](https://github.com/Uniswap/continuous-clearing-auction/blob/7d7602d257733315434570f2a0c2f94f1c7b207a/CHANGELOG.md).

CCA’s paper also retains strategic and timing considerations, including final issuance and boundary effects. Sealed-bid variants are discussed as future directions rather than established privacy properties of ordinary CCA bids. It would be inaccurate to translate a continuous animation into claims of universally fair allocation, bot immunity or private participation. The useful lesson is smoother discovery with explicit constraints and recoverable positions. [CCA paper](https://developers.uniswap.org/whitepaper_cca.pdf).

**Uniswap Instant Launch demonstrates sophisticated rights behind a simple interaction.** The documented strategy uses one billion 18-decimal tokens, a native-ETH pair, no hook, a 0.25% LP fee and spacing 25. It places the supply into a single-sided position held permanently by a fee splitter. Separate contracts support beneficiary NFTs, fee compounding and buyback/burn behavior. Those deployment choices should not become mandatory ANIMA economics. Their design lesson is that token ownership, withdrawable liquidity and transferable fee rights are separate objects. [Instant Launch](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/liquidity/liquidity-launchpad/concepts/instant-launch).

Compounding must cause a verified liquidity increase, not simply redraw a growing reserve. Uniswap’s documented collection and compounding flows distinguish collected fees from the subsequent reinvestment operation. ANIMA’s current redeemable position has no later share issuance or top-up method; copying a “compound” control onto it would require another position strategy or an explicit withdrawal-and-redeposit implementation. [Collect and compound fees](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/liquidity/liquidity-launchpad/guides/collect-and-compound-fees).

**Doppler’s main contribution is modular market formation.** Airlock coordinates token creation, governance, pool initialization and liquidity migration through approved modules. Its current SDK separates EVM and Solana entrypoints and exposes continuing operations such as fee-claim previews. Module compatibility and chain deployment are concrete constraints: an available SDK method does not prove that every associated contract exists on every network. [Airlock architecture](https://github.com/whetstoneresearch/doppler/blob/main/docs/Airlock.md), [Doppler SDK](https://github.com/whetstoneresearch/doppler-sdk), [Deployment inventory](https://github.com/whetstoneresearch/doppler/blob/main/Deployments.md).

Doppler Dynamic adjusts a v4 liquidity curve by time and sales progress. Epoch processing happens through activity; behind-schedule sales and ahead-of-schedule sales affect the curve differently. Its positions serve sellback, near-term distribution and further price discovery. Two demand sequences with the same final volume need not produce the same path. A faithful experiment must process those state transitions, rather than move an attractive curve with a time slider. [Dynamic auction mechanics](https://github.com/whetstoneresearch/doppler/blob/main/docs/Doppler.md).

Multicurve configurations distribute quantities over several ranges, with compatibility requirements such as connected ranges and normalized shares. Migration is another adapter decision: the documented no-op continuation is unavailable for Dynamic auctions, and fee-streaming migrations require compatible lockers and beneficiaries. These are candidates for future exact integrations, not features supplied by ANIMA’s present single-position wrapper. [Multicurve examples](https://docs.doppler.lol/reference/examples/multicurve), [Migration options](https://docs.doppler.lol/core-concepts/liquidity-migration-options).

Doppler’s Rehype routes fee assets toward buybacks, beneficiaries and LPs, with version-specific controller powers. This is a concrete meaning for economic “alchemy”: usage produces fees, and explicit rules transform those assets into defined outcomes. It still requires trading activity, executable conversion, custody and claim operations. Neither a fee route nor a buyback guarantees price appreciation. [Rehype documentation](https://docs.doppler.lol/advanced-features/rehype-pools).

**LBPs and overflow sales reveal why a visual model must show assumptions.** For project balance \(T\), reserve balance \(R\), project weight \(w\) and reserve weight \(1-w\), weighted-pool spot price in reserve per project token is

\[
p=\frac{R}{T}\frac{w}{1-w}.
\]

Changing weights alters that price, but actual trades also change the balances. A declining no-trade path does not imply every participant will receive a declining executed price. Balancer’s current LBP documentation also distinguishes owner funding windows and optional restrictions on selling the project token back. These exit rights belong beside the chart. [Balancer LBP](https://docs.balancer.fi/concepts/explore-available-balancer-pools/liquidity-bootstrapping-pool/liquidity-bootstrapping-pool.html), [Weighted math](https://docs-v2.balancer.fi/reference/math/weighted-math.html).

Fjord’s virtual-reserve variant reduces the creator’s upfront reserve requirement. The virtual balance remains a calculation input, while actual contributions fund real obligations. Its creator and participant documentation also distinguishes closing the sale, claim delays and vesting. Time passing makes an operation eligible; somebody still submits the required transaction. [Zero-liquidity LBPs](https://help.fjordfoundry.com/fjord-foundry-docs/for-sale-creators/fjord-features/zero-liquidity-lbps), [Closing and claiming](https://help.fjordfoundry.com/fjord-foundry-docs/for-sale-creators/fjord-features/token-and-liquidity-claiming).

For a proposed fixed-price overflow model, if total contributions are \(C\), the cap is \(H\), a participant contributes \(c_i\), and price is \(p\), then accepted funding is \(A=\min(C,H)\), accepted contribution is \(a_i=c_iA/C\), refund is \(c_i-a_i\), and purchased tokens are \(a_i/p\). A failed minimum raise instead returns the contribution under the specified failure rule. This is a model specification, not ANIMA’s existing Genesis batch formula: that contract rejects contributions exceeding its hard cap and distributes tokens pro rata over the final accepted raise. Gnosis’s funded limit-order auction is different again, including marginal partial fills and separate cancellation deadlines. [Fjord creation surface](https://app.fjordfoundry.com/token-sales/create), [Gnosis settlement implementation](https://github.com/gnosis/ido-contracts/blob/main/contracts/EasyAuction.sol).

**The archive already contains substantial executable capability.** The source review identifies three main launch mechanisms and an additional real hook system. Their main properties are:

| Archive component | Implemented behavior | Boundary to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| `GenesisLaunchpad.sol` | Token creation, native contributions, contribution reduction, success/failure settlement, market seeding, locks and claims/refunds | Default market is Genesis `NativeMarket`, not Uniswap |
| `GenesisV4Launchpad.sol` | Atomic fixed token, initialized and funded v4 pool, redeemable position shares | Explicitly hookless; standard ERC20 quote; no fundraising campaign |
| `ContinuousClearingAuction.sol` | Funded inventory, streamed lots, standing bids, checkpoints, cancellation and pull claims | ANIMA-specific algorithm; existing token; 64 active orders |
| `OwnerV4FeeHook.sol` | Actual configurable swap fees backed by PoolManager claims | Separate opt-in integration; exact-input/full-fill semantics |
| `OwnerFeeRouter.sol` | Chosen recipient weights, deposited claims, owner configuration and beneficiary withdrawals | Output-asset conversion requires an actual converter |
| `TimeVault.sol` | Cliff or linear release with fixed beneficiary rules | Locking project tokens does not automatically lock LP principal |
| `VestedExitVault.sol` | Funded scheduled sell slices and permissionless eligible execution | Existing market ABI requires an adapter for the direct v4 router |

The decisive failure in the earlier presentation was reducing that breadth to a designer. Existing `web/v4/client.mjs`, `public-session.mjs`, `desk.mjs`, the privacy worker and `web/confluence/wallet.mjs` already supplied significant transaction plumbing. Their presence did not automatically connect all mechanisms to the new studio, but it provided a working foundation that should have been used.

**Genesis batch fundraising has precise, restrictive economics.** `contracts/src/protocol/GenesisLaunchpad.sol` creates a fixed-supply token and a launch room. Participants contribute native currency during a fixed window and can reduce their contribution before close. Settlement is permissionless after close. Missing the minimum raise makes each recorded contribution refundable; success seeds its installed market, creates founder and treasury locks, and enables participant token claims.

For founder fraction \(f\), liquidity fraction of funding \(l\), supply \(S\) and final raised amount \(C\), the implementation first computes founder tokens, then approximately

\[
S_{public}=\frac{S-S_{founder}}{1+l},\qquad
S_{LP}=S-S_{founder}-S_{public},\qquad
C_{LP}=lC.
\]

The code applies integer floors. A participant receives its contribution fraction of `publicTokens`. This links contributor pricing to initial pool pricing within rounding. Independent allocation sliders cannot override that relationship while still claiming to execute this contract.

The archive restricts founder allocation to 0–20%, liquidity funding to 50–100%, and vesting to 30–1,825 days. Founder release has a 30-day cliff; treasury funds use a cliff lock ending with the vesting term. These are current contract requirements, not universal launchpad principles or choices imposed by the new interface. Removing them requires a separately specified contract change. The default `NativeMarket` seed has no ordinary LP withdrawal path. The experimental `V4GenesisMarket` and `PhoenixLaunchHook` are separate installation choices with a hardcoded fee ramp; they should not masquerade as the fully configurable owner hook.

**The direct v4 path is already an actual launch transaction.** In `integrations/console/protocol/v4-hook/src/GenesisV4Launchpad.sol`, deterministic token and position creation, PoolManager initialization, quote funding and liquidity seeding occur atomically. The fixed token has no subsequent mint function, transfer tax, blocklist or token administrator. Unused budgets return to the payer. `GenesisV4Position` exposes transferable ERC20 shares in one fixed range; holders redeem their proportional principal and ordinary accumulated LP fees. There is no later issuance, reseeding or arbitrary administrator sweep.

This route accepts standard ERC20 quote currency, so native ETH must be wrapped. The selected fixed LP fee is capped at 100,000 ppm by this factory. It is not a campaign with participant fundraising, founder vesting or automatic community distribution. Most importantly, its original pool key hardcodes `hooks=address(0)`. The router itself accepts hook data, but the previous ordinary browser swap builder selected no hook. Those facts explain why merely enabling a fee designer could never affect the resulting pool.

Pool initialization and funded liquidity are distinct protocol operations. v4 PositionManager has its own action and settlement semantics; fee collection is expressed through the appropriate zero-liquidity decrease and receiving balances, not a universal `COLLECT` action. ANIMA’s custom share wrapper uses the real PoolManager directly and does not claim to be the official position NFT. [Create a v4 pool](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/guides/create-pool), [PositionManager](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/guides/position-manager), [Collect v4 fees](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/guides/managing-liquidity/collect-fees).

**Prices must retain their units all the way into execution.** A human price is usually quote tokens per launched token. The pool instead stores a square-root ratio of its address-sorted raw currency units. For an 18-decimal launch token, quote decimals \(d\) and human quote price \(P\), the raw quote-per-token ratio is \(P10^{d-18}\). If the launch token sorts first, that ratio determines the stored square root; if it sorts second, the ratio is inverted. The result is encoded at Q96 precision. The interface can show a friendly price, but execution must resolve token addresses and decimals before calculating the final range and liquidity.

A price interval also reverses in raw pool coordinates when token order reverses. This is why the new clients predict the token first and then map human lower/upper prices onto correctly ordered, spacing-aligned ticks. Six-decimal quote assets and eighteen-decimal quote assets cannot share unadjusted arithmetic. `web/v4/math.mjs` provides integer square roots, tick math and budget calculations, and the new chain tests exercise both token orders. A range described as “narrow” is not enough: the review must show the actual represented interval, allocated inventory, unused funds and whether the starting price lies inside it.

**ANIMA’s streaming auction is its own bounded mechanism.** `contracts/src/extensions/markets/ContinuousClearingAuction.sol` sells an existing token in integer lots, with a fixed seller, reserve price and block schedule. Funding must arrive before the start. Standing bids escrow quantity multiplied by their limit price. Checkpoints clear newly available supply against active orders, with price priority and sequence priority for equal limits. A new bid cannot capture supply already elapsed before it arrived.

Its 64 active slots are a real capacity limit. Cancellation checkpoints first and uses the expected order sequence to prevent cancelling a reused slot. Token and refund claims are pull balances. Seller proceeds can become claimable during the auction; there is no minimum-raise guarantee. Unsold inventory and unspent escrow resolve at the end. There is no automatic token factory, DEX graduation, LP custody strategy, private bid system or participant vesting attached to this contract. The new interface must make these properties understandable rather than relabel the mechanism as Uniswap CCA.

**Fee rights are real, but their timing matters.** The existing `OwnerV4FeeHook` takes a chosen rate from gross input, in the input currency, and mints PoolManager ERC6909 claims. For gross input \(G\) and chosen ppm rate \(h\), the hook fee is \(\lfloor Gh/1{,}000{,}000\rfloor\); the remaining input reaches the pool, whose ordinary LP fee is separate. Its quote data carries a maximum accepted hook fee and deadline. Unsupported exact-output trades and incomplete fills revert.

The hook records the recipient and direct/splitter mode when a fee accrues. Anyone can flush only to that recorded destination. `OwnerFeeRouter` accepts 1–64 unique recipients with positive weights, creates independent withdrawable claims, and preserves already deposited claims when weights change. However, splitter weights apply **when the hook fee is flushed into the splitter**, not at the earlier swap. The interface must display that distinction. A promise that every swap snapshots final beneficiary weights would require different versioned allocation or immutable policy.

`depositConverted` and `claimConverted` are existing extension points, but a production conversion route was not supplied for arbitrary output assets. A recipient’s preferred payout token therefore cannot be treated as an executed conversion merely because its address is stored. A working conversion must name the actual input, adapter, route, minimum output, deadline and recipient. v4 custom-accounting rules likewise require real settlement rather than detached fee percentages. [v4 custom accounting](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/guides/custom-accounting), [Dynamic LP fees](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/concepts/dynamic-fees).

**Imported projects are useful references, not automatically connected functions.** The archive’s `integrations/github/cutting-edge` includes `AgentLaunchpad`, virtual reserves, early caps, a decaying tax and a graduation interface. Its actual liquidity deployer still needs a compatible implementation, and its prescribed fee legs differ from the requested owner-defined economics. `integrations/github/most-advanced` includes `Kiln`, deterministic token creation and hook recipes; `DeskLaunch` explicitly does not build complete v4 liquidity calldata. `integrations/dave` provides distinct basket and house-market protocols. Their ideas can be adapted deliberately. Importing their folders or displaying their names does not establish operational integration, and their runtime assumptions should not be silently mixed with Genesis.

**The new edition now connects the missing operational paths.** `web/launchpad/chain.mjs` supplies a shared public wallet transaction workflow. It verifies deployment bindings, resolves actual quote decimals and address ordering, prepares infrastructure when required, resets existing over-budget approvals, requests exact new budgets, simulates the next operation, estimates gas and binds the review to the account, chain and transaction. Submission stores the hash before waiting for confirmation, enabling recovery after a reload without sending a duplicate. Confirmed launch events establish actual token, position and pool identity. Position reads expose shares, current pool state and redeemable assets.

The new `GenesisV4HookLaunchpad.sol` is a separate opt-in factory. It reuses `GenesisFixedToken` and `GenesisV4Position`, adds an explicit hook argument, and places that address in the actual funded PoolKey. Its deterministic namespace also includes the chosen hook. The original launchpad, router, token, position and owner-hook ABI and executable bytecode remain unchanged. `web/launchpad/hook-client.mjs` prepares deployments, asynchronous cancelable CREATE2 address mining, hooked launches, real-quoter-backed swaps, fee forwarding, beneficiary claims and owner configuration. It pins known runtime code and validates manager and owner/recipient scope. The factory’s compatibility checks alone are not an audit of an arbitrary caller-selected hook.

`web/launchpad/sale-client.mjs` connects the existing Genesis fundraising lifecycle. It verifies the launchpad installed in a sealed ledger, matching NativeMarket and TimeVault modules, and pinned-block state. It prepares creation, contribution, withdrawal, settlement and claim/refund calls; reads personal entitlements and actual founder/treasury locks; and supports explicit infrastructure setup where needed. Mature founder and treasury locks can be released permissionlessly to their contract-fixed beneficiaries; the caller cannot redirect that release. Ordinary-wallet operations use identity zero and do not falsely claim NFT ownership. A separate NFT-account custody flow remains a distinct authorization context.

`web/launchpad/auction-client.mjs` wraps the existing ANIMA auction in human units and full lifecycle operations: deployment, exact inventory approval/funding, bids, cancellation, checkpointing and all claim types. Reads include the previously omitted `lotSize`, own standing bids, claim balances and current phase. Verification checks the known executable runtime and consistent values at every immutable reference. It feeds unsigned plans into the same reviewed wallet workflow rather than introducing another hidden signing path.

These adapters are executable source in the new edition. A finished GUI must still expose them coherently, preserve their state across navigation, reconcile receipts and make recovery usable. At this report snapshot, that integration is underway. Publishing a hosted interface would not itself rewrite immutable NFT bytecode or change the runtime URI of an already minted token. Any minted edition must follow its own verified renderer, storage and upgrade permissions.

**The owner’s controls should follow human intent while preserving exact execution.** The primary creation question is whether the owner wants an immediate market, a community raise or the supported standing-bid auction. From there, use ordinary token quantities, funding amounts, prices, recipients and dates. Reveal raw ticks, ppm, block numbers and contract addresses when they help an advanced user verify or change the plan. They should not be the price of understanding the basic workflow.

The same persistent launch should have creator and participant perspectives. Creator-only authoring can remain inside the NFT’s owner workspace, while public participation requires an explicit reachable entry for contributors. Keeping the whole hosted workspace private does not by itself produce a usable public campaign. The participant surface needs terms, live phase, personal contribution or bids, received allocation, refundable amount and claim readiness without exposing creator-only controls.

Token allocation, treasury, LP principal and fee claims should appear as separate rights. Each must name its controller, beneficiary, releasable amount, relevant schedule and change authority. OpenZeppelin’s finance components illustrate why “vesting” alone is insufficient: later deposits can follow an existing schedule, and beneficiary rights may themselves be transferable. A claim operation should not silently withdraw principal; a rebalance should explicitly describe what is removed, received and redeployed. [OpenZeppelin vesting reference](https://docs.openzeppelin.com/contracts/5.x/api/finance), [ERC20 capability reference](https://docs.openzeppelin.com/contracts/5.x/api/token/erc20).

**Privacy is several capabilities, not one decorative toggle.** An encrypted draft protects stored configuration. Shielded funding changes the funding path. Private memory protects message content. A public pool still exposes its token pair, price, balances, timing and external events. RAILGUN cross-contract execution temporarily moves assets into Relay Adapt, performs ordered calls and reshields the defined outputs atomically. The output list and exact approvals must cover recoverable assets and leftovers. [RAILGUN cross-contract calls](https://docs.railgun.org/developer-guide/wallet/transactions/cross-contract-calls.md).

The actual private submission workflow includes estimation, broadcaster fees, proof generation, population and submission. Changed inputs or expired quotes can require fresh work. Public self-submission and public recipients can introduce links that encrypted local storage cannot remove; shielding also has a public entry transaction. The product should explain scope where the owner chooses funding and destinations, and a requested private route must not silently become public. [RAILGUN private transaction UX](https://docs.railgun.org/developer-guide/wallet/transactions/ux-private-transactions.md), [Community broadcasters](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/learn/privacy-system/community-broadcasters.md), [Shielding](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/learn/shielding-tokens.md).

The archive contains a separate private direct-v4 path with proof/submission machinery. The newly added hooked launch route has not yet been composed into that private RelayAdapt/proof flow. Genesis public fundraising and ANIMA public bids also do not become confidential through draft encryption. The appropriate interface offers only execution modes supported by the selected adapter and retains the original private path’s explicit behavior.

**Useful alchemy is the visible transformation of conserved assets and rights.** The strongest next capabilities are economically specific:

1. **Allocation conservation:** changing team allocation, fundraising terms or liquidity budget immediately recomputes everything they constrain. No asset is promised twice, and every remainder has a destination.
2. **Market continuity:** compare the actual participant acquisition rule with the pool’s opening price and funded depth. Show a mismatch before launch rather than hide it behind FDV.
3. **A lifecycle experiment:** apply a reproducible event sequence to the chosen mechanism—low demand, oversubscription where supported, late orders, settlement, claims and unlocks. The same scenario must retain the same launch and balances.
4. **Fee anatomy:** show gross input, hook charge, net pool input, ordinary LP fee, output and recipient accrual. Compare policies using a user-entered trading path rather than invented future volume.
5. **Unlock pressure:** show how much supply becomes transferable at each real cliff or release date, then let the owner explicitly model sales against actual reserve assumptions. This is a stress case, not a price prediction.
6. **Living ownership:** connect the NFT controller, treasury, vesting recipients, liquidity shares and fee beneficiaries. The visual should reveal enforceable relationships and who can change them.

Advanced future work can add exact CCA or Doppler adapters, multiple positions, explicit compounding, conversion and versioned fee allocation. Each addition should have a complete lifecycle and transaction evidence. A renamed approximate model is weaker than a narrower mechanism whose money and rights remain correct from creation through final withdrawal.

**The remove/change priorities are concrete.** Remove configuration export as the terminal “launch” outcome, detached hook selectors that never reach PoolKey, payout-token labels without conversion, and success animations before successful receipt reconciliation. Keep readable export as an optional inspection tool. Remove arbitrary studio-only economic limits; where a contract really has bounds, show them and name the chosen mechanism. Remove automatic claims of privacy from encryption and claims of Uniswap equivalence from ANIMA-specific auction math.

Change the studio into a persistent operating surface with creation, participation, positions, fees, claims and activity. Reuse the archive’s precise integer math and authorization infrastructure. Preserve the user’s selected object and interaction controls while fixing the launch lifecycle. Separate current capabilities from imported experiments. Keep the actual chain, deployment version, token powers and custody discoverable. Ethereum’s RPC state reads, simulation and receipts support that workflow, but simulation remains a read at a particular state until a transaction is mined. [Ethereum JSON-RPC](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/apis/json-rpc/).

The work should be accepted on observable behavior: an owner can prepare and sign the supported launch, a participant can exercise the mechanism’s real rights, funds remain conserved through success and failure, fees reach their chosen beneficiaries, and the interface recovers pending transactions without inventing outcomes. Beauty can then express the structure of a real system instead of compensating for operations that stop at a form.
